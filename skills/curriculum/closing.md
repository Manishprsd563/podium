# Closing

## Why it matters
The last sentence is the one the audience carries out of the room. A talk
that just stops when the slides run out throws away the most memorable
moment it had.

## What good looks like
- A final sentence that explicitly restates the talk's one takeaway, not a
  summary of every point covered.
- Delivered with the same energy as the rest of the talk, not a trail-off
  or an apologetic "so, yeah, that's it".
- Short: under 15 words, so it lands cleanly.
- No new information is introduced in the close -- it reinforces what was
  already said.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: The talk ends when the last slide's content runs out, with no distinct closing sentence.
Speaker: Has a closing sentence, but it summarizes everything instead of naming the one takeaway, or trails off in energy.
Presenter: One clear, short takeaway sentence, delivered with intent.
TEDx: A short takeaway sentence that echoes the hook's stake, closing the loop the talk opened.

## Drill
Say your closing line again in under fifteen words, and make sure it
answers the question your opening line raised.
