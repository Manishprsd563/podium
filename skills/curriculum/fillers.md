# Fillers

## Why it matters
"Um", "uh", and "like" cost nothing individually, but a listener's ear
starts counting them once the rate climbs -- and every one is a half-second
where no new information reached the audience.

## What good looks like
- Vocal fillers (um, uh, mm, hmm, mhm, er) under 2 per minute: clean.
- 2-5 per minute: normal, no penalty alone.
- Over 5 per minute: distracting enough to cap delivery at a 3.
- Verbal crutches (like, you know, basically, actually, sort of, kind of, i
  mean, right) under 3 per minute: fine.
- Fillers do not cluster -- three in one sentence is worse than one every
  thirty seconds even at the same average rate.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Fillers appear in most sentences, often clustered when reaching for the next thought.
Speaker: Under 5 per minute on average, but still clusters under pressure.
Presenter: Under 2 per minute, evenly spread, none in the opening or close.
TEDx: Near zero, replaced by a silent pause instead of a filler when reaching for the next thought.

## Drill
Say your next sentence again, and every time you feel a filler coming,
replace it with one full second of silence instead.
