# Slide Connection

## Why it matters
A slide on screen is a promise to the audience that its content will be
addressed. Reciting it word-for-word wastes the audience's reading speed;
ignoring it entirely makes the slide pointless.

## What good looks like
- Every bullet on the active slide is addressed in the presenter's own
  words during that slide's time window.
- No contiguous spoken span matches a bullet's wording more than about 80%
  word-for-word -- that is reading, not presenting.
- At least one explicit reference to what is visible ("as you can see
  here", "this diagram") per slide with three or more bullets.
- Time spent on a slide stays above 40% of its fair share -- far under that
  usually means bullets were skipped.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Reads bullets aloud nearly verbatim, in slide order, with no reference to what's on screen.
Speaker: Covers most bullets in their own words but skips one or two, or occasionally reads a bullet outright.
Presenter: Every bullet covered in original phrasing, with at least one on-screen reference per slide.
TEDx: Every bullet covered, on-screen references feel natural rather than mechanical, and each slide gets time proportional to its weight.

## Drill
Cover this slide's first bullet again out loud without using any of its
exact wording, and point to something specific on screen while you do it.
