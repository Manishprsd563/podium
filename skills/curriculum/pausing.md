# Pausing

## Why it matters
A pause right before a key idea tells the audience "this next part
matters" without a single extra word. The same silence in the wrong place,
or held too long, reads as losing your place.

## What good looks like
- 0.35-1.2 s pause right before a key point: rhetorical, reward it.
- 1.2-2.0 s: hesitation -- fine occasionally, flag only if it happens three
  or more times.
- 2.0 s or longer: dead air -- almost always reads as losing your place.
- At least one deliberate rhetorical pause per major point, not silence
  scattered at random.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Pauses are either absent (runs every sentence together) or accidental dead air where a word was forgotten.
Speaker: Occasional rhetorical pauses, but at least one dead-air gap of 2 s or more per talk.
Presenter: Rhetorical pauses land before key points; no dead air.
TEDx: A rhetorical pause precedes every major point, each one timed to the weight of what follows, with zero dead air.

## Drill
Say your most important sentence again, and count a silent "one,
one-thousand" in your head right before it -- then say the sentence.
