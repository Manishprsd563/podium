# Structure

## Why it matters
A roadmap tells the audience how many stops the talk has, so they can
budget their attention; transitions keep them on the map as the talk moves;
a close that restates the takeaway is what they actually remember.

## What good looks like
- One roadmap sentence naming 2-4 stops, right after the hook.
- An explicit verbal transition at every slide or topic change ("now",
  "next", "so that brings us to").
- A close that restates the takeaway in one sentence, not a trail-off.
- No slide spends more than 1.5x its fair share of time while another sits
  under 0.5x -- an unbalanced talk feels structurally broken even if every
  part is present.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: No roadmap; slide changes happen with no verbal signal; the talk just stops when the slides run out.
Speaker: States a roadmap but skips it in delivery, or signals some slide changes and not others.
Presenter: Roadmap, transitions, and a real close are all present, with minor time imbalance across slides.
TEDx: Roadmap, transitions, and close land cleanly, and time is spent in proportion to each slide's importance, not its order.

## Drill
Say your roadmap sentence out loud in under eight seconds: "Today I'll
cover X, then Y, then Z" -- if it takes longer, cut a stop.
