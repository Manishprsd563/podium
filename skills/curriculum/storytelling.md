# Storytelling

## Why it matters
An audience remembers a concrete example days after they forget a formal
definition. A talk that states only the abstract version of an idea leaves
nothing for the listener to hold on to.

## What good looks like
- Every formal or technical claim is paired with a concrete example, a
  short analogy, or a plain-word restatement, before or right after it.
- The intuitive version comes first when the idea allows it; the formal
  version lands second, once the audience already has the picture.
- Concrete language ("imagine you're...", "like when...") appears at least
  once per major point, not just once in the whole talk.
- Examples are specific -- a named scenario, not "for example, in some
  cases".

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: States only the formal or technical version of each idea with no example anywhere.
Speaker: One example somewhere in the talk, but most points stay abstract.
Presenter: Most major points get a concrete example or analogy, roughly in order.
TEDx: Every major point has its own concrete image, and the images build on each other across the talk.

## Drill
Say your most abstract sentence again, then immediately add "-- imagine
you're..." and finish with one specific, concrete scene.
