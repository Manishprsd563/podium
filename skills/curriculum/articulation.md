# Articulation

## Why it matters
When a speech recognizer can't confidently resolve a word, a portion of a
live human audience likely had the same trouble -- this is a proxy for how
clearly the talk's own vocabulary came through, not a judgement of accent.

## What good looks like
- Intelligibility (share of words the recognizer resolved with confidence
  0.6 or higher) at 0.95 or above.
- Deck-specific terms in particular are resolved cleanly -- these are the
  words the audience most needs to catch.
- No deck term appears in the low-confidence list more than once across the
  talk.
- Described to the presenter only as "the recognizer had trouble with X",
  never as a mispronunciation or accent issue.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Multiple deck terms repeatedly land in the low-confidence list, including ones that only recur once and are never resolved cleanly later.
Speaker: A handful of low-confidence terms, mostly resolved correctly on later mentions.
Presenter: Intelligibility 0.85 or higher; any low-confidence terms are one-off, not deck terms.
TEDx: Intelligibility 0.95 or higher; deck terms are never flagged.

## Drill
Say this slide's key term three times at a slightly slower, more separated
pace than the rest of your talk, then say the full sentence again at normal
speed.
