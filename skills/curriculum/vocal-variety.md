# Vocal Variety

## Why it matters
A flat volume and pitch across an entire talk reads as monotone, even when
the words are strong -- variation is what tells the ear which words carry
the weight.

## What good looks like
- Loudness variance of 2 dB or more across the talk: normal, healthy
  variation.
- Under 2 dB: monotone, flag it.
- The loudest moments line up with the talk's actual key points, not random
  spots.
- Quieter, closer-in delivery on an aside or caveat; louder, more open
  delivery on the main claim.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Same volume and energy from the first word to the last, key points sound identical to filler sentences.
Speaker: Some natural variation, but it doesn't track which sentences matter most.
Presenter: Loudness rises on key points and eases on asides, variance above 2 dB.
TEDx: Variation is deliberate and matched to meaning -- softer to draw the audience in, louder to land the point, with a pause bridging the two.

## Drill
Say your key sentence three times: once quiet and close, once at your
normal talking volume, once with real lift -- pick the one that actually
sounds like you mean it.
