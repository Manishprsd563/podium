# Pacing

## Why it matters
Speed controls how much a listener's brain can absorb in real time. Too
slow and the talk feels padded and under-rehearsed; too fast and the
audience loses the thread mid-sentence.

## What good looks like
- 110-160 words per minute: the conversational target band, no penalty.
- Under 110 wpm: sounds under-rehearsed or padded with dead air.
- 160-185 wpm: acceptable only if rhetorical pauses are still present at key
  points.
- Over 185 wpm: rushed -- listeners lose the thread.
- Pace stays close to even across slides; no single slide is delivered at a
  dramatically different speed than the rest without a reason.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Speeds up or slows down unconsciously, often rushing the slides they know best and stalling on the ones they don't.
Speaker: Mostly in-band, with a few slides that spike above 185 wpm under pressure.
Presenter: Consistently 110-160 wpm across the whole talk.
TEDx: 110-160 wpm with a deliberate brief slowdown right before the single most important sentence.

## Drill
Say the next thirty seconds of your talk again out loud while counting a
slow one-two beat between phrases -- if you run out of material before
thirty seconds, you were rushing.
