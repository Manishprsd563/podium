# Hook

## Why it matters
The first two sentences decide whether the room leans in or checks out. A
talk that opens with the topic name instead of the stakes has already lost
the audience's attention before the first slide loads.

## What good looks like
- States why the topic matters, or the question it answers, in the first
  two sentences -- not just the topic's name.
- Lands before any roadmap or agenda sentence.
- Uses a concrete stake, a question, or a surprising fact, not "today I will
  talk about...".
- Fits in one breath: under 20 words.

## Novice → TEDx
Novice: Opens with the topic name and moves straight into slide one.
Speaker: States the topic and adds one reason it matters, but takes two or three sentences to get there.
Presenter: Opens on the stake or the question in the first sentence, topic name second.
TEDx: Opens on a concrete stake or question that makes the audience feel the answer matters to them personally, in under 20 words.

## Drill
Give me your opening line again, in under ten seconds, and end it on the
question the audience should be asking themselves -- not on your topic's
name.
